Kidney Stones: Causes, Symptoms & Treatment

Kidney Health · 2 Jul 2026

Kidney stones form when minerals and salts in the urine crystallise into hard deposits inside the kidney. They are one of the most common reasons people visit a urologist, and while a small stone may pass unnoticed, a larger one can cause sudden, severe pain.

Why Stones Form

Low fluid intake, certain diets, family history and some medical conditions all increase the risk of stone formation. Stones vary in composition — calcium, uric acid and other types each respond slightly differently to prevention strategies.

Recognising the Symptoms

  • Sudden, sharp pain in the back or side
  • Pain radiating towards the lower abdomen and groin
  • Blood in the urine
  • Nausea or vomiting alongside the pain

How Stones Are Treated

Small stones often pass on their own with hydration and pain relief. Larger stones may need a procedure such as ureteroscopy with laser fragmentation (RIRS), or PCNL for bigger kidney stones. The right approach depends on the stone's size, location and composition.

Preventing Recurrence

Once you've had one stone, you're more likely to form another. Adequate water intake, dietary adjustments and, in some cases, medication can meaningfully reduce that risk — a plan best built around your specific stone type.
