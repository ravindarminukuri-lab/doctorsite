What Is Kidney Cancer?

Kidney cancer, most often renal cell carcinoma, begins in the cells of the kidney tissue. Tumours vary in size and behaviour, and early-stage disease is frequently asymptomatic — meaning many are found incidentally during imaging done for another reason.

Signs & How It's Often Found

  • Blood in the urine
  • A lump or persistent ache in the flank or lower back
  • Unexplained fatigue or weight loss
  • An incidental finding on ultrasound or CT done for another reason

Diagnosis

Diagnosis is typically confirmed with a CT or MRI scan, which also helps map the tumour's size and position relative to the kidney's blood vessels — key information for planning whether the kidney can be partially preserved.

Treatment Options

  • Robotic partial nephrectomy — removing the tumour while preserving healthy kidney tissue, where feasible
  • Robotic radical nephrectomy for larger or centrally located tumours
  • Active surveillance for very small, low-risk tumours in select patients
  • Management of complex renal tumours involving major blood vessels

Recovery & Outlook

Recovery after kidney cancer surgery depends on whether the procedure was partial or radical, and whether it was performed robotically or as open surgery. Robotic procedures often allow earlier mobilisation and a shorter hospital stay, followed by structured monitoring of kidney function.

  • Early mobilisation encouraged after robotic surgery
  • Kidney function monitored closely during recovery
  • Regular imaging follow-up to watch for recurrence
